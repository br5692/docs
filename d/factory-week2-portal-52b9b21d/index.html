Week 2 the partner path

What a partner touches. A public application form, a review and decision inside Salesforce, an email, and a sign-in that uses no password. Every step below is proven end to end in the partial sandbox on real email. The foundation it runs on is in the Week 1 reference.

1

Applying

proven

A public page. No account, no login, no invitation. One submission decides between three outcomes before anything is written.

Submission arrivescompany, website, name, email, partner type
↓
Is this email already known?yes → refuse, write nothing, record existing_application
↓ no
Is this website domain already known?yes → attach a second person to that company
↓ no
Create the Account and the Contact togetherstatus Applied, applied-at stamped, primary contact set

Low level

  • The Account and the Contact are written in one composite call, so a half-made partner cannot exist.
  • The partner type dropdown is served from Factory’s own picklist, read live from the org. It cannot drift from theirs because it holds no copy.
  • If the email domain differs from the website host, one sentence is appended to the note for the reviewer. It blocks nothing.
  • All three outcomes return the same message to the applicant. The real reason is in the audit row, where only staff can read it.

Review and decision

proven

Decisions happen where Factory’s team already works. Two paths reach the same three field writes.

Partner Applications tabpending list and the decision panel, two screens
Account recordApprove or Deny quick action
↓
The same three writesstatus, accepted-at, contract state, in one save
↓
A validation rule closes every other routeApproved is refused while the primary contact is blank

Low level

  • Both paths require a primary contact, and a validation rule closes every other route to Approved, including an inline edit or a data load.
  • The page and its permission are ours. Factory’s own Account page layouts were never edited; the four quick actions are appended after their own.
  • A user without the review permission does not see the tab at all.
  • Four queues sit on the page: applications pending, applications decided, access requests, access decided.

3

The email

proven on real email

The email is triggered by the status field, not by the button. Whatever sets the field sends the message.

Status becomes Approved or Deniedby the panel, the quick action or a direct edit
↓
A record-triggered flow firesonly when the field changes to meet the condition
↓
Email to the primary contact, plus one audit rowapproval invites them to request a link

Low level

  • The approval email carries the portal address rather than a link, because a flow cannot mint a sign-in token.
  • The decision note is internal and appears in neither email.
  • Two failure modes are visible in the log rather than silent: no recipient, and a send the org refused.
  • Mail is sent from a Salesforce-authenticated address today. Which mailbox partners should see is an open question for Factory.
4

Signing in

proven

There is no password for a partner to create, forget or have stolen.

Partner types their emailsame reply whatever the address
↓
Link minted, hash stored, expiry setfifteen minutes, single use
↓
Link opened on the portalhash matched, both fields cleared, last sign-in stamped
↓
Signed session cookieseven days, carries the identity the rules check

Low level

  • The link itself is never stored. Only a one-way hash of it, which cannot be reversed into a working link.
  • The email link lands on the portal first, so the browser reaches the portal’s own address before any token is exchanged.
  • Scoping is enforced by the service on every call, not by the page. A partner asking for another partner’s data receives nothing.
  • Deactivating a partner in Salesforce cuts access immediately.

5

The dashboard

shell until Week 4
  • The partner’s first name and their company name in the header.
  • Navigation: Dashboard, Register a deal (present and disabled), Sign out.
  • A registrations list, which is empty and says so. It fills in Week 4 when partners can register deals.
  • Typing the dashboard address without a session returns the sign-in page.

The empty state is deliberate. What this step proves is that a partner reached their own dashboard with no password and can see their own account and nobody else’s.

6

Several people, one company

proven

A company decision and a person decision are two different things.

First person appliesapproving the company approves them too, one click
↓
A colleague applies at the same domainattached to the company, their own status Applied
↓
They are refused a sign-in linksame neutral message, recorded as no_approved_contact
↓
An operator approves the personaccess decision panel, reason recorded
  • Approving a company approves whoever applied for it, so the common case stays one click.
  • Everyone after the first is approved individually. An approved company does not let a new person straight in.
  • Each person decision writes one audit row carrying the reason.
  • The access queue is empty most of the time. That is the healthy state.

Rules enforced today

RuleHow it behaves
One application per emailA repeat from the same address gets the same neutral reply, writes nothing, and records existing_application.
Match the company before creating oneA new email at a website domain already known attaches a person to that company rather than creating a second one.
Never reveal who is a partnerAn unknown address gets the identical reply to a known one, on the form and on the sign-in page.
A hidden field catches botsAny value in it returns the same reply, writes nothing, and records honeypot.
Approval needs a recipientA validation rule blocks Approved while the primary contact is blank, on every path including a direct field edit or a data load.
Both must be approvedSign-in requires the company approved and the person approved.
A link works onceFifteen minutes, single use, stored only as a one-way hash and cleared the moment it is used.
Requests are cappedFive per email an hour, twenty per address an hour, answered with a retry time that reveals nothing.
