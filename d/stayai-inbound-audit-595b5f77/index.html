Inbound audit: routing, speed to lead, scoring and channel performance

Covers six areas of the inbound motion: lead routing configuration, time to first touch, lead scoring coverage, the demo request path, channel performance by win rate and revenue, and the resulting fix list. All figures cover the 12 months to 22 September 2026.

01

Routing

broken

The risk raised on the call: someone asks to talk to sales, and nobody follows up.

There is no active lead routing. One assignment rule exists in the entire org, New Partner Assignment, and it is inactive. No queue is used, so every lead is owned by an individual from the moment it is created.

130 leads have sat in status New for more than 90 days. 115 of them are owned by two deactivated users — Michael Yates (79, Leadership) and Amir Torabi (36, Partnerships). Both are IsActive = false. Those leads cannot be followed up, because their owners cannot log in.

slow and uneven

On the Lead object, 182 of 226 leads (81%) have no logged task ever. Of the 44 that were worked, the median response was 30 minutes. Leads that get picked up get picked up fast. Most are never picked up.

Demo booking is healthy. Median time from opportunity created to demo is 2 days, and 64 of 69 land inside a week. Demo dates only populate reliably since the Chili Piper flow went live on 09-14, so that sample skews recent.

03

Lead scoring

installed, unused

The capability is paid for and switched off.

  • Pardot is installed with pi__score__c and pi__Pardot_Last_Scored_At__c. Zero leads carry a score.
  • The standard Rating field is empty on all 226 leads, so there is no hot-lead flag.
  • LeadSource is blank on 220 of 226 leads, so lead-level attribution is unusable.
04

Demo request path

partly instrumented

294 Chili Piper demos booked in the window.

  • 95 attached to an Opportunity, 166 attached to an Account, 33 attached to neither.
  • 22 cancelled.
  • No-show reads zero across all 294 events. The field is not being written, rather than nobody missing a demo. Cancelled and no-show demos are the cheapest re-engagement list a team has, and half of it is invisible.

Separately, 46 of the 213 inbound opportunities sit on an account with no segment at all.

One limit worth stating. Whether every web form submission becomes a record at all cannot be answered from the CRM alone. It needs the form tool and the website analytics alongside it. Everything above measures what reached Salesforce, not what was sent.

The table comes from a grouped aggregate. Recomputing it row by row from all 590 raw closed records produces identical counts, rates and totals.

Partner carries 40% of all won revenue at the best win rate of any volume channel, and it is the only channel growing: 145 opportunities created in 2025 against 204 so far in 2026. Six of those 204 carry a partner-involved checkbox, and none of them name the partner. No opportunity in the org has a partner record attached. Events is the weakest channel at 22%, 13 wins against 47 losses, and it costs money to attend.

The CX figure is an artifact rather than channel strength. Those deals are only entered once they are known to close.

06

What to fix

The first three answer the routing risk directly. None require new tooling.

  • Reassign the 115 stranded leads off the two deactivated users. Same-day fix, no build.
  • Turn on lead routing. A queue plus an active assignment rule, so no lead is ever owned by nobody.
  • Set a follow-up expectation on inbound. 46 opportunities waited more than a week.
  • Decide on Pardot scoring. Switch it on and route hot leads first, or stop paying for it.
  • Fix the no-show field so cancelled and missed demos become a re-engagement list.
  • Capture the sourcing partner on the opportunity, agreed on the 09-22 call as low hanging fruit.
