Week 1 in one line

Week 1 is the foundation everything else sits on: the data model inside your org, a service that talks to it, a sign-in that uses no password, and a record of every single call.

If the call only has room for one sentence about Week 1, that is the sentence. The six items below are the same thing at more depth.

1

Data model

  • 47 fields across five objects, every one an addition. Nothing Factory already had was renamed, retyped or removed.
  • Partner Influence is their object, not a new one, and four of its fields came straight from their own metadata export. The Portal Audit Log is the only genuinely new object.
  • Every field carries help text in Setup, so their admin can read what each one does without asking us.

Show it Object Manager, Account, filtered on Portal or Partner. Full field table in the test script.

2

Certificate sign-in

  • No password exists anywhere in this system. The service holds a private key, and the matching public certificate sits on the connected app in their org.
  • Salesforce checks the signature every time the service asks for a session. The public half on its own is useless to anyone who copies it.
  • Valid to December 2028, and replacing it is a configuration change, not a code change. It signs in as one named API-only user, so every action traces back to that user.

Show it External Client App Manager, Partner Portal Tool Layer, Settings, OAuth Settings.

3

Tool layer

  • One service, two doors. The portal calls it over the web, an agent calls it over MCP, and both land in the same code, so the rules and the logging cannot drift apart.
  • Thirteen actions are live, driven from a single list that feeds both doors. Five more names are reserved for later weeks and refuse cleanly rather than erroring.
  • It stores nothing of its own. It reads and writes Salesforce and keeps no copy of their data.

Show it The health check, which reports its version and whether Salesforce is reachable.

4

Permissions and rules

  • One table maps five caller types to the actions each may call: anonymous, partner, staff, account executive, agent.
  • Applying is anonymous only. The dashboard is partner only. The review queue is staff only. Agents can read and check conflicts but can never make a decision.
  • A signed-in partner whose contract is not signed can only see who they are and their contract status. Every refusal is recorded, never silently dropped.

Show it Portal Audit Log with Outcome filtered to Denied.

5

Audit log

  • Every call writes exactly one row, including the ones that are refused. 516 rows sit in the sandbox today.
  • Outcomes are Success, Denied or Error. Almost every Error today is the notification email failing, because sandbox email is off by default. The Salesforce record was still written.
  • Nine columns, listed below. A partner links to their Contact, a staff member to their User, and only one of those is ever filled.

Show it Portal Audit Log, All.

6

Hosting

  • The portal runs on Cloudflare Pages and the service runs on Render. Both are in our accounts today.
  • The portal is a plain static site and the service is a container, so either can move to another host without a code change.
  • Nothing in the code knows which host it is on. Moving it into Factory’s own accounts is a handover of configuration, not a rebuild.

Show it Both addresses are live and listed in the test script.

Week 2 in one line

Week 2 is the part a partner actually touches: they apply on a public page, Factory approves them inside Salesforce, and they sign in with an emailed link and no password.

This is what runs live on the call. The four items below are the same thing at more depth.

7

Applying

  • Anyone can apply on the public page. One submission creates the partner Account and the Contact together, stamped Applied with the date and the partner type taken from Factory’s own picklist.
  • Duplicates are caught before anything is written. A known email gets the same neutral thank-you and nothing is created. A known website domain attaches the new contact to the Account that already exists instead of making a second one.
  • A hidden field catches bots. They get the identical thank-you, nothing is written, and the attempt is still recorded.

Show it The application page.

8

Review and approve

  • Operators work from a Partner Applications tab: a pending list and a decision screen, both behind a permission Factory controls. A user without it does not see the tab at all.
  • Approving stamps the status, the accepted date and the contract state, and saves an internal note explaining why. Denying does the same with its own note. The note is for Factory, never shown to the partner.
  • Approval fires a flow that emails the partner an invitation to sign in. None of this touches Factory’s own page layouts, which is why nothing of theirs had to change.

Show it The Partner Applications tab.

9

Sign-in

  • The partner types their email and gets a one-time link. There is no password for them to create, forget or have stolen.
  • The link is stored only as a one-way hash, expires after 15 minutes, and stops working the moment it is used once.
  • An unknown or unapproved address gets exactly the same message as an approved one, so the page cannot be used to discover who Factory’s partners are. Requests are capped at five per email and twenty per address an hour.

Show it The sign-in page.

10

The dashboard

  • After the link, the partner lands on a dashboard that greets them by name and shows their own account’s registrations and nothing else.
  • The session is a signed cookie valid for seven days. It carries the identity the permission table checks on every single call, so scoping is enforced by the service and not by the page.
  • Typing the dashboard address without a session sends them to sign-in. Setting their account to Inactive in Salesforce cuts access immediately.

Show it The portal.

Only one of Partner Contact and Salesforce User is ever filled. Which one tells you whether a partner or a member of staff made the call. The raw record ID is still on the record itself, under Record Touched, for tracing a support question.

The 47 fields, as five groups

Speak to the groups, not the fields. Each group has one purpose, and that purpose answers almost every question about it.

ObjectFieldsWhat the group is for
Account14The partner relationship: type, lifecycle stage, applied and approved dates, commission rate, contract state.
Contact3Sign-in only: last sign-in, link expiry, and a one-way hash of the link. The link itself is never stored.

Questions that are likely

QuestionAnswer
Did you change anything of ours?No. Every change is an addition. Nothing renamed, retyped or removed, and that is a standing rule on the build.
Where does the data live?In their sandbox. The service keeps no copy.
What if we pick a different esign tool?Two fields hold the external id and the signing link. Swapping tools is a webhook change. Their answer on which tool is still outstanding.
Can our own agents call this?Yes. That is the second door and it is already live.
Do partners need a Salesforce license?No. Partners are Contacts and never enter Salesforce. They only ever see the portal.
Is any of this in production?No. Everything so far is sandbox, by design.
Who can reach the portal today?Anyone with the address sees the landing and application pages. The dashboard needs an approved partner and an emailed link.
How do we know it works?166 automated test cases cover the service and the portal, plus a 61-step script a person runs by hand.

Say these plainly if they come up

  • Sandbox email is off by default, so notification emails only send while it is switched on for a session. The records are written either way.
  • The commission definition is still open. The field exists and stays empty until Factory defines it.
  • The esign tool is still open, and it is holding up the contract step.
  • Week 6 is specified but not approved, and nothing for it has been built.

Naming what is unfinished is what makes the finished parts believable. Do not soften these.
